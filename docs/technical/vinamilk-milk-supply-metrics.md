# Vinamilk milk-supply decision metrics

## Decision this domain supports

**Management question:** Is the fresh-milk sourcing mix and supplier network keeping enough dependable domestic supply while productivity, quality and sourcing costs remain controlled? The evidence supports a public-data prototype for sourcing structure and supplier-support coverage. It does not support operational procurement decisions on its own: supplier prices, accepted/rejected volumes, lab quality, factory demand and regional/monthly breakdowns are not disclosed in these reports.

## Candidate KPIs and their use

| KPI | Definition | What it helps decide | Current feasibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Household-sourced raw-milk volume | Purchased fresh raw milk from contracted/partner farmers in the fiscal year, normalized to tonnes | Whether contracted sourcing is growing or shrinking | Three annual report observations are available (2023–2025); verify consistent perimeter before publishing a trend |
| Household sourcing share | Household-sourced volume ÷ total fresh raw milk supplied × 100 | Whether supply is shifting between partner households and managed farms | Indicative values: 58.6% (2023, using 239,254/408,000), 55.6% (2024, using 227,000/408,000), 55.1% (2025, using 225,778.211/410,000). Denominators are approximate and 2024 is internally inconsistent; keep as **derived / needs review**, never a reported figure |
| Managed-farm supply share | Own-farm/Mộc Châu farm volume ÷ total fresh raw-milk volume × 100 | Whether to invest in managed farm capacity vs household partnerships | 2023 and 2024 have approximate reported components; not an independently audited productivity comparison |
| Farmer support coverage | Supported households ÷ households identified as needing support × 100 | Whether supplier capability/quality interventions reach the at-risk pool | 2025 reports 100% for households with substandard milk; denominator and count are not published |
| Supplier base and collection capacity | Active household count; operating collection points by operator/region | Where sourcing concentration or collection bottlenecks may exist | Public snapshots exist, but definitions and geography vary; do not infer a time trend without reconciliation |
| Feed-cost support | Difference between Vinamilk-supplied feed price and a defined local market basket | Whether input assistance improves farmer economics and supply stability | 2025 report says 3–5% below market average; market basket, region, dates and distribution are absent |
| Milk yield per cow | Accepted raw milk volume ÷ average productive cow-days (or lactating cows) | Benchmarking productivity and capacity investment | Reports disclose annual group volumes and some herd counts, but not aligned average productive-cow denominators; do not calculate from year-end herd alone |

### Decisions this evidence can support

The strongest current use is to **choose what to investigate or pilot next**, not to authorize a budget shift from public reports alone:

- Compare domestic and overseas/export growth, then prioritize an export-market margin, capacity and working-capital review; the faster growth is a diligence signal, not proof of higher return.
- Prioritize a product-level unit-economics review of plant-based and Green Farm, then run a controlled distribution or promotion test if internal margin and repeat-purchase data support it.
- Use the >96% OEE attainment and reported savings to ask factory owners for the remaining line-level exceptions and savings calculations; public data does not identify which plant to invest in.
- Investigate the household-milk volume trend before changing supplier commitments, because disclosed totals have different scopes and the 2024 report itself gives both 402,000 and approximately 408,000 tonnes.
- Use the 83-person net headcount increase to trigger a workforce-plan review; compare it with vacancies, turnover, role mix and output per employee before changing recruiting targets.

### Live decision-support example (FY2025)

**Question:** Given domestic and overseas revenue growth in 2025, which market should management investigate first for incremental investment?

| Market | FY2024 net revenue | FY2025 net revenue | FY2025 reported YoY growth | Source |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Domestic | VND 50,799 billion | VND 50,964 billion | 0.3% | Annual Reports 2024 and 2025, PDF p. 6 / p. 46 |
| Overseas | VND 10,983 billion | VND 12,682 billion | 15.5% | Annual Reports 2024 and 2025, PDF p. 6 / p. 48 |

Overseas growth exceeded domestic growth by **15.2 percentage points** (calculated from the reported rates). The reported revenue amounts increased by **VND 165 billion domestically** and **VND 1,699 billion overseas** from FY2024 to FY2025 (calculated from the verified annual values); FY2025 domestic revenue was still about **4.02×** the overseas-market amount. This supports prioritizing an overseas-market diligence review while protecting the much larger domestic revenue base; it does **not** by itself justify shifting investment. Before allocating capital, management still needs segment gross margin, market-specific selling/distribution cost, working capital, capacity constraints, risk and expected incremental return. The live `/api/ask` path now queries these growth and revenue observations from role-authorized verified SQL data and cites their exact report pages/chunks; it does not use unrelated retrieved passages as evidence.

These are concrete management questions tied to reported evidence. The answer should state the evidence, the next decision gate, and the missing internal measure; it should not turn a growth figure into an unsupported recommendation to move money.

## Decision question supported by the verified milk-purchase series

**Question:** Is household-farmer milk procurement shrinking enough to warrant a sourcing-network review?

| Fiscal year | Purchased from farming households |

| 2023 | 239,254 tonnes |

The reported volume fell approximately **5.12%** from 2023 to 2024 and **0.54%** from 2024 to 2025, or **5.63%** across 2023–2025. This supports investigating whether the change reflects supplier count, volume per supplier, collection coverage, accepted-milk quality or reporting-scope differences. The sourcing-share calculations above are arithmetic on published rounded values, included to show the dashboard question the evidence could answer. They are not approved KPIs until the report perimeter and denominator are reviewed. Avoid mixing purchased household milk, total raw-milk supply, fresh-milk production and total dairy-ingredient consumption as though they were the same measure.

## Additional decision domains extracted from official reports

The LLM-backed parser in `src/core/analytics/business_metric_extraction.py` extracts review candidates from parser-produced table and narrative chunks only for the explicitly selected `BUSINESS_DECISION_PROFILES` domain. Generic numeric/layout signals choose chunks; the model receives only that domain's management question and primary metric allowlist. For `market_performance`, this is domestic/overseas revenue scale and growth; export growth remains supporting evidence to avoid duplicating near-overlapping growth measures in the default decision view. The full catalog remains available for dashboard analytics and admin entry. Server validation rejects metrics outside the selected profile, and accepted values require an exact quote, unit and period from a source chunk. These are candidates only; an admin must verify each value and its scope before SQL analytics can use it.

Examples that can support decisions after review:

- **Export investment:** 2024 domestic net revenue was VND 50,799 billion (+0.4%), versus VND 10,983 billion overseas (+12.6%); reported exports were VND 5,664 billion (+12.4%). The 2025 Annual Report's domestic revenue of VND 50,964 billion (+0.3%) and overseas revenue of VND 12,682 billion (+15.5%) are source-chunk matched and admin verified. This supports prioritizing a market/portfolio review, but not an investment decision without segment margins, capacity and market-level costs.
- **Domestic versus overseas economics:** the audited geographic table in the 2023 annual report gives domestic and overseas net revenue and segment gross profit for both 2022 and 2023. This lets the dashboard compare segment gross-profit growth and calculate gross margin by year; it omits selling, administration, capital and market-level costs, so it can prioritize further diligence but cannot justify reallocating budget on its own.
- **Factory improvement:** the 2019–2023 TPM summary reports productivity +14%, OEE +10% on line A1, energy costs −4%, steam −20%, and VND 113 billion raw-material savings. These support investigating where TPM practices can transfer; line-level baselines and capex are still needed to approve expansion.
- **Water, emissions and waste:** the 2025 sustainability report provides 2023–2025 values for water intake/consumption, Scope 1/2 emissions and waste. Waste totals are explicitly affected by an expanded 2025 assurance boundary, so do not interpret the 2024–2025 jump as an operational deterioration without reconciling scope.
- **Farm and route-to-market capacity:** the 2024 report states 14 domestic dairy farms, 130,000 managed cows, 80 raw-milk collection stations (scope differs from the Annual Report’s 87 operator-count sum), about 11,187 supermarket/convenience-store points of sale and about 630 Vinamilk stores. These are scale indicators, not utilization or productivity measures.
- **Manufacturing, workforce and portfolio (2025):** the Annual Report reports VND 115.7 billion raw-material savings, 92% of production overhead budget used, 9,906 employees and more than 358,000 training hours. The product count is unresolved: printed p. 46 says 100 products launched/relaunched, while p. 45 says more than 100. Those two product-count candidates remain pending; do not treat the discrepancy as a verified trend.

### Dashboard and decision-agent flow

`/api/analytics` returns only role-visible, verified KPI rows. The dashboard defaults to primary decision KPIs within one chosen business domain, allows an explicit all-metrics view, and shows a decision-question shortcut only when that domain and role have every required metric. Domain/visibility filters affect the dashboard display, not SQL query availability. `SQLAnalyticsAgent` queries the authorized verified-metric views and links result rows back to their metric evidence; `ReportAgent` builds source-cited decision summaries for segment economics, market growth, export-country prioritization, factory efficiency, farmer milk sourcing, product portfolio and environmental trends. If an explicitly requested KPI has no role-visible verified row, the report agent abstains instead of substituting a similar number from retrieved document text. Source page, indexed chunk and quote remain on each cited metric for review.

The live provenance audit on 2026-10-01 found 131 verified business observations and 12 verified financial observations. All 143 link to an existing indexed chunk, have a source-content hash, and match that hash against the stored chunk. Migration `20261001140000_backfill_verified_financial_metric_chunks.sql` restores the seven legacy FY2020–2021 financial links by exact quote or unique same-page label/value match.

Each candidate retains its physical PDF page, exact source quote, extractor/prompt version and source-chunk hash. `source_chunk_id` points to the indexed `document_chunks` row used by retrieval, and admin review can inspect that chunk and its page. ## Data needed for an actionable operating dashboard

The annual/sustainability reports are enough for an evidence-backed strategic overview, not day-to-day supplier decisions. To answer “where should we change contracts, support or capacity?”, the business would need monthly observations with source-system provenance for:

- supplier/farm/collection station and receiving factory (with access controls for non-public data);
- ordered, delivered, accepted and rejected kilograms, by date and product grade;
- contract price and effective cost per accepted kilogram, including transport and cooling;
- lab results (fat, protein/SNF, SCC/TPC, antibiotic residues), rejection reasons and quality thresholds;
- milking, collection and arrival timestamps plus temperature excursions;
- active/lactating cow counts and cow-days for yield comparisons;
- supplier training/support dates, feed assistance volume/cost and follow-up quality outcomes;
- factory demand/production plan and inventory to calculate coverage, shortage risk and purchase-vs-capacity scenarios.

These are requirements for a future internal data source, **not values to infer from public PDFs**. Until supplied, the UI should label this domain as public-report coverage and display “not available” for operating cost, quality, yield and demand KPIs.

Source quotes are validated against the exact parser chunk before a candidate can be saved. `--max-pages` remains as a CLI alias for `--max-chunks`. These profiles are a curated decision scope and need full-report benchmarking plus domain-owner review; historical manually reviewed observations remain database evidence, not output claims for this extractor.

Each preloaded, parser-extracted or manually entered observation enters review with its source and scope; only a verified observation feeds analytics. The verified-period unique index keeps partial-year and full-year observations distinct and blocks conflicting values from appearing together in one period.
